# Post types

How each kind of input becomes posts. The channel files decide the voice and the final say on what fits. This file covers the defaults.

The goal is a steady, credible presence on every channel with very little time spent, not virality. One input should feed several channels over several weeks.

## Quick map

| Type | Input | Media | Best channels | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feature launch | One screenshot and a short note | The screenshot | Every channel, including employees | Company channels on launch day, people over 1 to 2 weeks |
| News | A link to an article | Screenshot of the headline | LinkedIn, X, founder profiles | Same day or next day. Then never |
| Podcast or video clips | An episode cut into 20 to 30 shorts | One clip per post | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, X | One or two clips per channel per week over about 3 months |
| Customer review | Screenshot of a review | The screenshot | LinkedIn first, then company X and Facebook | At most one a week per channel |
| Event photos | A few photos and what happened | The photos | LinkedIn (company and people), Instagram | Before, on the day, and a recap within 2 days |
| Data or text insight | A number, chart, or opinion | A simple visual made for the post | LinkedIn, X, Threads, Instagram (needs the visual) | One channel a day across a week |
| Article or long-form promotion | A blog post, guide or report | Cover image or a pulled quote card | Every channel | 3 to 6 posts per channel over 2 to 3 weeks, each with a different angle |

## Feature launch

Input is usually one screenshot and one message about what changed and who it's for.

- Company LinkedIn and X: what it does, who it helps, where to find it. Launch day or the next working day.
- Founder profiles: why you built it, a customer request behind it, or what it says about where the product is going. A few days later.
- Sales and marketing employees: what it means for the people they talk to. Spread over the following 1 to 2 weeks so the feeds don't all light up on the same morning. These need the employee's approval.
- Instagram and TikTok: only if there's a visual worth looking at. Otherwise skip.
- Small fixes and minor releases: one company post, or fold them into a monthly "what's new" post instead of a post each.

## News

Show the market you follow your industry. Input is a link.

- Media is a screenshot of the headline and the first lines, cropped clean. Don't screenshot paywalled text beyond the headline and standfirst.
- The post gives your take in two to four sentences: what it means for your audience, whether you agree. Credit the outlet and link the article where the platform allows it.
- Same day, or next day at the latest. Stale news posts look worse than none.
- At most two or three news posts per channel per week. If the week already has that many, drop the weakest one rather than pushing it to next week.

## Podcast or video clips

One episode becomes 20 to 30 shorts. At several channels each that is a few hundred posts, so spreading them well matters more than any single caption.

- Each clip gets its own record per channel, with the video in `Media` and a caption built on the clip's own point, not the episode summary.
- Order: the strongest 3 to 5 clips in the first two weeks, the rest over about 3 months. On any one channel, keep clips from the same episode at least 3 days apart.
- Vertical video channels (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) take the most clips. LinkedIn and X take the best third.
- Mention the guest and link the full episode in the caption or first comment, as the channel file says.
- YouTube needs a video. Instagram and TikTok reject text-only posts. A clip without its file yet is `Needs media`.
- Editing the clips is still manual. Ask for the files, or create the records as `Needs media` with the clip timestamps in `Source`.

## Customer review

Reviews from Trustpilot, G2 or app stores do well, especially on LinkedIn.

- Media is a screenshot of the review. Crop out the reviewer's surname or photo unless the review is public under their full name.
- The post thanks the customer and adds one line on what they used or why it matters. Don't rewrite or embellish the quote.
- LinkedIn company page first. X and Facebook optional. Founder and employee profiles occasionally, and only if the channel file allows social proof.
- At most one review post a week per channel, so it never reads like an ad campaign.

## Event photos

- Before the event (if you know in advance): one post per channel that will attend, about a week out and again the day before.
- On the day: one photo post on the company channel and the profile of the person speaking or attending.
- After: a recap with 2 to 4 photos within 2 days. LinkedIn takes carousels. Instagram takes up to 10 images per post.
- Name people only if they're in the photo and it's normal for them to be tagged. Don't guess names.

## Data or text insight

A number from your product, a benchmark, a short opinion. These need a visual on image-only platforms and do better with one everywhere.

- Make a simple visual: one number or one chart, a short title, the source line, brand colours. See `references/visuals.md`. Don't use AI image generation for charts. Render them from the data so the numbers stay correct.
- Text-first channels (LinkedIn, X, Threads) can run the text alone if the channel file prefers it.
- Spread one insight across channels over a week rather than posting it everywhere on one day.
- Double-check every number against the source before the post goes to `To approve`.

## Article or long-form promotion

A blog post, guide, report or toolkit can feed a channel for weeks.

- Write 3 to 6 posts per channel, each with a different angle: the main finding, a surprising detail, a practical tip, a quote, a question to the audience.
- Spread them over 2 to 3 weeks and mix them with other types. No channel should run two promotional posts on consecutive days.
- Link the article in every post where the platform shows links well. On Instagram, point to the bio link instead.

## Mixing types

Across any week on a channel, aim for a mix of education or insight, engagement (question, opinion), product (feature, how-to), and social proof (review, customer story, event). The strategy file may set its own ratio. When it does, follow it.

When the calendar is already full of one type, push the new post to a lighter week, or tell the user it's better skipped.
