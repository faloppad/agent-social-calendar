## Purpose

My profile does two jobs. Installers who meet me at a fair or on a call look me up, and they should see someone who knows their trade. Founders, investors and candidates should see a company that's growing and a CEO who says what she thinks. Serves Objective 2.

### Goals for this channel

- 3 posts a week.
- Get through the Ground Floor episode 58 clips by end of January, about one a week.
- Followers 4,100 → 5,500.
- At least two posts a month that an installer would forward to another installer.

## Audience

- Installer company owners, many of them customers or prospects.
- Other founders, especially people building software for trades.
- Investors, including our own.
- People who might want to work at Brightloop.

## Voice

First person. I ran operations at my uncle's HVAC company for five years before Brightloop, and I write like it: direct, a bit dry, specific about how jobs actually get done. I have opinions and I say them, but I don't lecture. If I made a mistake, I say so plainly. I don't do inspirational.

### Do

- Start with something that happened, a number, or a thing someone said to me.
- Short paragraphs. One-line paragraphs are fine, but not every line.
- Say what I'd do differently. People trust that more than wins.
- Use real numbers when I'm allowed to share them.
- Mention Jonas or the team by name when they did the work.

### Don't

- No "I'm humbled". No "lessons I learned" lists with 7 bullet points.
- Don't turn a story into a moral at the end. If the point is clear, stop.
- Don't pitch Brightloop in personal posts. Link to it at most once a month.
- No hot takes about AI unless it's about how we actually use it.

### Phrases that sound like me

- "Honest answer: ..."
- "I kept a spreadsheet."
- "I wouldn't recommend it as a plan."
- "That's the part nobody puts in the deck."

### Banned on this channel

- "Founder journey", "building in public"
- "Hustle", "grind"
- "Unpopular opinion" as an opener

### The fundraising pillar

Most of my "Company and founders" posts this quarter are about the seed round. People ask about it, and it brings in candidates and other founders. What I'm happy to share: amount (€1.6M), lead (Harbourline Ventures), pre-money (€6M), how many funds said no (31 of 38), how long it took (about 4 months), and how we set the valuation.

On the valuation: before the first investor meeting we ran our numbers through Equidam (https://www.equidam.com) and used the valuation report as our starting point. When Harbourline opened lower, we went through the report's inputs with them instead of arguing about a number. Fine to mention when a post is about how we priced the round. Don't force it into posts about anything else, and don't make it sound like an ad. It's a tool I used, said the way I'd say it to another founder.

## Example posts

### Example 1: founder story

```
Ten years ago I was the planner at my uncle's HVAC company in Dordrecht. 22 technicians, one whiteboard, one of me.

Every Monday at 6:45 I rebuilt the week. By Tuesday lunch it was wrong again: a job overran, a part didn't arrive, a customer wasn't home.

I didn't think of it as a software problem. I thought I was bad at planning.

It took me three years and a lot of conversations with other planners to realise everyone was rebuilding their week twice. That's still the most useful thing I know about our customers.
```

Why it works: specific place, specific time, an honest admission, no lesson tacked on.

### Example 2: opinion from experience

```
We lose deals when we demo too many features.

Jonas and I listened back to 20 demo recordings last month. In the ones that closed, we showed three things and spent the rest of the time on the prospect's actual week. In the ones that didn't, we showed everything.

What an installer wants to see in a demo is next Monday, calmer. Show them that and stop.
```

Why it works: a claim, the evidence behind it, and a short end.

### Example 3: podcast clip

```
Sam asked me on The Ground Floor what I'd do differently with our seed round.

Start three months earlier. And spend those three months listening to investors, not pitching them.

We went into our first meetings with a deck built for the investors we imagined. The questions we actually got were about churn and about whether installers would ever pay more than €50 a month. We'd have had better answers if we'd heard those questions in February instead of May.

Episode 58: https://thegroundfloor.example/58
```

Why it works: one question, one answer, the detail behind it.
